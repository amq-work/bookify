"We were aiming to build a **realistic, portfolio-grade SaaS product**, not a fictional “future technology” concept and not another flashy website.

## What the project is about

The project is a **branded appointment-booking SaaS for small businesses**.

The idea is simple:

A business comes to the platform and tells it:

* What their business/niche is
* Who their target customers are
* What services they provide
* What kind of appointments/bookings they take
* What information they need from customers
* How they want their booking experience to behave

The SaaS then creates a **recommended booking form/experience** for that business.

The business can then customize it and publish it.

The final booking experience can be used through:

**1. Public link**

> `yourapp.com/book/business-name`

**2. Embedded booking form**

> A generated embed code that developers can place inside the business's existing website.

So the product is essentially:

> **A customizable, branded booking-experience builder for small businesses.**

It is not primarily trying to compete with Calendly on scheduling. The interesting problem is **giving small businesses control over how their booking experience looks, feels, and collects information.**

---

# Why we chose this project

This is a **personal portfolio showcase project for you**, not a commercial product you necessarily need to launch as a real company.

The purpose is to demonstrate that you can:

**Research a real problem → understand users → define the product → design the experience → build the frontend → implement real backend logic.**

We specifically wanted something that proves more than:

> “I can make beautiful UI.”

We want the recruiter to see:

> “This person understands users, thinks about products, understands business requirements, and can actually build what they design.”

---

# What we decided before development

### 1. The product must be based on a real market category

We rejected completely fictional/futuristic concepts.

Appointment booking already exists as a real market.

Our differentiation comes from **solving a specific problem inside that market**, rather than inventing an entirely new category.

---

### 2. The project should not be unnecessarily complex

We deliberately did **not** want to build:

* Calendly
* HubSpot
* Salesforce
* A massive CRM
* Enterprise scheduling infrastructure

The project should remain small enough to actually finish.

Our focus is:

**UI/UX + user research + frontend + some serious backend knowledge.**

---

### 3. The core experience starts with business understanding

Instead of:

> Sign up → dashboard → figure everything yourself

we decided on:

**Business information → niche → target audience → services → booking requirements → recommended form**

That recommendation can initially be generated through **rule-based logic**, not a paid AI API.

For example:

**Dental clinic + family audience**

could produce recommended fields such as:

* Name
* Phone
* Email
* Appointment type
* Preferred date/time
* Reason for visit

The business can then change everything.

---

### 4. Customization is a major part of the product

The business can customize:

**Branding**

* Logo
* Colors
* HEX palette
* Fonts

**Form**

* Questions
* Field types
* Required/optional fields
* Ordering
* Sections
* Multi-step/single-page layout
* Conditional questions

**Content**

* Heading
* Description
* CTA
* Success message
* Instructions

**Appearance**

* Form style
* Radius
* spacing
* button style
* visual presets

And the changes should be visible through a **live preview**.

---

### 5. We decided to build the logic before the final UI

This is important.

You said you wanted to **focus on the UI only after the logical and technical foundation is complete**.

So development priority is:

**Product logic → database → workflows → booking engine → configuration → authentication → validation → states → then visual polish.**

We're not spending the first phase making fancy animations.

---

### 6. It must be genuinely functional

The portfolio project should actually work.

A complete core flow should be:

**Business signs up**

→ completes onboarding

→ creates services

→ sets availability

→ gets recommended booking form

→ customizes it

→ previews it

→ publishes it

→ receives public booking

→ appointment is stored

→ business manages appointment in dashboard.

---

### 7. Zero-budget constraint

We decided the entire portfolio project should be buildable with:

**Next.js / React / TypeScript**
**Supabase / PostgreSQL / Auth / Storage**
**Vercel**
**GitHub**
**Free/open-source libraries**

No dependency on:

* Paid AI APIs
* Paid infrastructure
* Paid SMS
* Paid WhatsApp APIs
* Stripe
* expensive third-party services

For things like AI-style recommendations, telemetry, or other external functionality, we can use **rule-based logic, mock data, or simulated functionality** where appropriate.

---

We're trying to prove:

> **“Give me a real business problem and I can understand it, research it, design a product around it, and turn that product into a working system.”**

That is the actual purpose of this portfolio project.
